Key West Cruise Port Guide

Start with docking, not a checklist

Key West is a compact Florida Keys cruise call. Old Town, Mallory Square and Duval Street sit close together compared with large city ports — yet the first practical fact is still where the ship comes alongside. That changes walking comfort, whether a short shuttle appears useful, and how ambitious the rest of the day should be.

No invented assignments. This guide names common docking contexts for orientation. It does not publish cruise-line pier schedules, guaranteed shuttle rules, or fixed walking times for every berth.

Docking arrangements to understand

Mallory Square area

Mallory Square is the well-known harbourfront gathering place associated with sunset watching and the denser tourist edge of Old Town. When a ship is positioned in this general waterfront cluster, many passengers orient on foot toward harbourfront streets and Duval Street without a long transfer.

Outer Mole

Outer Mole is a more separated docking context. Expect a longer walk into Old Town or a short local transfer before the densest streets begin. Conch Train, trolley, taxi and pedicab options are often discussed here as practical support rather than as “must-buy tours”.

Pier B and other central berths

Other central harbour arrangements, including Pier B and related waterfront berths, may be used depending on operations. Treat them as distinct from a Mallory-only mental map. Confirm the berth named for your sailing instead of assuming one “main pier” for Key West.

Walking vs transport

Use the dedicated walkability guide for docking-scoped walking notes. In short: walking is often realistic for Old Town interests; longer landmarks and heat change the calculation; sit-and-ride sightseeing can help when you want coverage with less continuous walking.

Return planning

  • Know published arrival, all-aboard and departure times for your sailing
  • Leave margin before you stretch to farther photo stops
  • Do not rely on generic “operators usually allow 60–90 minutes” claims — policies differ

How docking changes the day

A Mallory-adjacent call often supports a walk-first Old Town shape. An Outer Mole call may spend early minutes on approach or transfer before the same streets feel close. Either way, Key West usually rewards one clear plan — see one-day styles — more than a packed landmark list.

Practical basics

  • Currency is US dollar; cards are widely used in Old Town
  • English is the everyday language at the port and in visitor areas
  • Getting around: walking, pedicabs, taxis, and local sightseeing transport

Questions about this page

Does this guide assign cruise lines to specific piers?

No. Docking arrangements change. Treat named locations as planning context, then confirm your sailing’s berth and any shuttle notes from your ship or port information.

Is Key West a long-transfer port?

Usually not. Compared with many Caribbean city calls, Key West keeps Old Town relatively close — but “close” still varies by docking point and by how far you walk into the island.
